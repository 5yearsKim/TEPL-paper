TEPL first-draft source audit — checked 2026-10-19

Scope: external claims were checked against primary papers, project documentation,
and source code. This is a focused check of cited claims, not a systematic novelty
review. No prior-system performance numbers are used in the paper.

1. tensorright
   Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3704865
   Author artifact: https://github.com/ADAPT-uiuc/TensorRight
   Checked: arbitrary-rank/size tensor rewrite verification; aggregated axes as
   possibly unbounded sets of named axes. Author list and publication metadata
   cross-checked with the publisher-deposited Crossref record.
   Used in Sections 2, 5.1.1, and 8; grouping is explicitly credited as prior work.

2. egg
   Author paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03082
   Author PDF: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~cnandi/docs/popl21-cr.pdf
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3434304
   Checked: equality saturation, symbolic rewrites, e-class analyses, extensibility.
   Used for framework context, without claiming it cannot represent attributes.

3. egg-applier
   https://docs.rs/egg/0.11.0/egg/trait.Applier.html
   Checked: Applier can inspect analysis data and construct replacements.
   The latest page identifies version 0.11.0; the versioned URL also returned
   successfully by direct HTTP retrieval. Inference boundary is the paper's
   interpretation of this API, not a quoted absence-of-feature theorem.

4. xla-simplifier
   Revision: 9cb4e2005f963acc873dc95facd3b7d134dbf108
   Revision date: 2026-10-17
   https://github.com/openxla/xla/blob/9cb4e2005f963acc873dc95facd3b7d134dbf108/xla/hlo/transforms/simplifiers/algebraic_simplifier.cc
   Primary raw source retrieved directly from raw.githubusercontent.com at that
   revision; revision selected through GitHub's commits API for the file.
   Checked AssociativeReorderNestedDot, beginning at line 3830: construction of
   new dimension descriptors, profitability checks, graph construction, and a
   possible transpose/output permutation. No line count attributed to attribute
   logic. Code attribution/classification remains TODO before an effort metric.

5. spores
   https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p1919-wang.pdf
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.14778/3407790.3407799
   Checked Section 2.3, Definition 2.4: indexed atoms, summed products, and
   weighted polynomial terms; relational translation and optimization.
   The paper PDF identifies volume 13, issue 11 and pages 1919–1932.
   Crossref's issue field says 12; references.bib follows the primary PDF's 11.
   TEPL does not inherit SPORES's completeness claims.

6. taso
   Author PDF: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~zhihaoj2/papers/sosp19.pdf
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3341301.3359630
   Checked abstract and substitution-generation/verification discussion:
   generating candidate graphs, operator properties, formal checking, cost-based
   search. Metadata checked against publisher-deposited Crossref record.
   No broad claim that TASO lacks parameter handling or arbitrary-rank reasoning.

7. tensat
   https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2021/file/cc427d934a7f6c0663e5923f49eba531-Paper.pdf
   Official record:
   https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/cc427d934a7f6c0663e5923f49eba531-Abstract.html
   Official BibTeX: https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/335-/bibtex
   Checked Sections 1–3: equality saturation and extraction for graph optimization;
   Table 2 includes stride/axis parameters and variable-length permutations.
   BibTeX confirms volume 3, pages 255–268 (2021). Full author names follow PDF.

8. souper
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04422
   Checked the authors' abstract: synthesizing superoptimization for LLVM.
   Cited as the verified 2017 arXiv report, without inventing conference metadata.

9. ruler
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10436
   Author record: https://ztatlock.net/pubs/2021-oopsla-ruler/
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3485496
   Checked: equality saturation for rewrite rule inference; metadata cross-checked
   with publisher-deposited Crossref record (including author order).

10. mlir-linalg
    https://mlir.llvm.org/docs/Dialects/Linalg/
    Checked: scalar payloads and indexing maps between iteration and operand
    spaces in structured operations. The citation is to documentation accessed
    in 2026; it does not assert a measured or historical comparison.

11. mlir-drr
    https://mlir.llvm.org/docs/DeclarativeRewrites/
    Checked sections on attribute capture, NativeCodeCall, and custom op building.
    Used to explain explicit host callbacks, without claiming their presence
    prevents richer attribute reasoning in other systems.

12. numpy-einsum (checked 2026-10-25)
    https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.einsum.html
    Official NumPy API documentation. Checked subscript labels as operand
    dimensions, repeated-label summation, explicit output ordering, and the
    matrix-multiplication interpretation used in Section 4.1.

Still unresolved: systematic novelty/expressiveness comparison; shared task
coverage with related verifiers; exact numerical-policy alignment; benchmark
selection and effort metrics. These remain explicit TODOs in the paper.

Related Work revision — checked 2026-10-27

TensorRight: https://lsrcz.github.io/files/POPL25.pdf
  Rechecked Sections 4–6: users supply both sides and preconditions;
  aggregated axes compactly represent arbitrary ranks; sufficient rank bounds
  reduce verification to finite proof obligations. Section 5 uses unordered
  named axes for layout-insensitive reasoning and relabeling for axis matching.
  The TEPL comparison concerns ordered positional attribute construction, not
  a claim that TensorRight cannot express attributes or verify transpositions.

MLIR DRR: https://mlir.llvm.org/docs/DeclarativeRewrites/
  Rechecked compilation to C++ rewrite patterns and NativeCodeCall attribute
  transformations. No blanket claim that all attributes require native code.

13. mlir-pdll
    https://mlir.llvm.org/docs/PDLL/
    Checked declarative matching, AOT/JIT pattern compilation, native constraints,
    and native rewrite functions. Documentation accessed 27 October 2026.

14. axon
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26344
    https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26344v1
    Checked author list and June 2026 submission metadata; abstract and Section
    4.4 describe instruction synthesis from semantic specifications. Cited as a
    preprint. Comparison identifies TEPL's synthesis output, without claiming
    that Axon lacks instruction-parameter or attribute handling.

TASO, TENSAT, SPORES, Ruler, and MLIR Linalg were rechecked at the primary
URLs above for the concise comparisons in Section 11. SPORES's algebraic
completeness is distinguished from TEPL's attribute-synthesis completeness.

Compiler optimization context — checked 2026-10-27

15. tvm
    https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi18/presentation/chen
    https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~arvind/papers/tvm.pdf
    The abstract reports production use; Section 3 describes constant folding,
    operator fusion, and layout transformations. Fusion reduces intermediate
    memory traffic. Used as evidence of practical graph transformations, not
    as a claim that all TVM optimizations are declarative tensor rewrite rules.

16. xla-hlo-passes
    https://openxla.org/xla/hlo_passes
    Official documentation describes HLO optimization passes and algebraic
    simplification. The opening paragraph lists optimizations across XLA and
    TVM collectively, without attributing every example to both sources.

TASO and TENSAT abstracts and introductions rechecked for performance gains
from searching equivalent graphs through substitutions. Their detailed
system comparison was replaced with a brief joint citation in the opening
paragraph; no production deployment claim is made for TASO or TENSAT.

Grouped optimization citations — checked 2026-10-27
  The opening now groups research by optimization purpose. SPORES appears
  only in the graph-optimization group, not the semantic-representation
  comparison. Production-use wording remains attached to XLA and TVM.

17. unity
    https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/unger
    Publisher abstract and BibTeX: joint algebraic transformation and
    parallelization through graph substitutions for distributed training.

18. mirage
    https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi25/presentation/wu-mengdi
    Publisher abstract and BibTeX: algebraic and schedule transformations
    spanning kernel, thread-block, and thread levels. Cites OSDI 2025 Mirage,
    not the later Mirage Persistent Kernel system.

19. trinity
    https://ina.kaist.ac.kr/assets/bibliography/Trinity.pdf
    Published ASPLOS 2026 paper, abstract and first-page citation metadata:
    tile-level equality saturation jointly optimizes algebraic equivalence,
    memory I/O, and compute orchestration; bridges graph and operator levels.

20. dnnfusion
    https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13342
    PLDI 2021 author manuscript: mathematical-property-based graph rewriting
    facilitates operator fusion. Bibliography uses the published paper DOI
    printed on page 1, not the unrelated resource DOI on its arXiv landing page.

21. neptune
    https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08726
    https://github.com/uiuc-arc/neptune
    PLDI 2026 paper and author repository: advanced reduction fusion uses
    algebraic correction expressions. First-page citation gives article 220,
    37 pages, DOI 10.1145/3808298. Cited for algebraic transformations supporting
    fusion, without claiming its method uses predefined graph rewrite rules.
