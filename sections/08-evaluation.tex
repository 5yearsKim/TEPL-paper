\section{XLA Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
\sidefigure[0.45]{figures/XLA_rewritie_cases.pdf}{XLA rewrite case results. Percentages use the immediate parent population. The completeness guarantee additionally requires the theorem's stated assumptions.}{fig:xla-results}{A horizontal tree partitions 209 XLA rewrite cases into 123 semantically supported cases and 86 without semantic support. Of the supported cases, 38 have RHS axis fields: 31 structural theorem candidates are inferred and verified, and seven outside the theorem scope remain unresolved. Of the 85 cases without RHS axis fields, 67 verify and 18 remain unresolved.}
We evaluate 209 XLA rewrite cases~\cite{xla-evaluation-source}. Figure~\ref{fig:xla-results} summarizes semantic coverage and the
outcomes of axis inference and verification.


TEPL provides the operation semantics required for evaluation in 123 of the
209 cases (58.9\%). The remaining 86 cases require semantic support that is
not yet available. Semantic support allows TEPL to attempt verification. A case succeeds when
any requested axis fields are inferred and the resulting rewrite is proved
legal and equivalent under its written conditions.

Of the 123 supported cases, 38 contain RHS axis fields. TEPL infers and
verifies 31 of these cases (81.6\%), completing all requested axis fields.
These cases are structural theorem candidates; the completeness guarantee
applies only under Section~\ref{sec:formal-scope}'s assumptions, including
input extents of at least two. Singleton cases may still be verified,
but inference is not guaranteed complete for them.
All seven unresolved cases involve slice or convolution, which fall outside
the theorem's scope (Table~\ref{tab:direct-scope}, D1). These results do not
imply that axis inference is impossible.

The other 85 supported cases have no RHS axis fields to infer. TEPL
verifies 67 of them (78.8\%), while 18 remain unresolved. These cases
measure verification rather than axis inference. Equivalence is verified
under the operations' declared semantics, assuming exact algebraic arithmetic.

We also evaluate token counts for the 31 successfully inferred XLA rewrite
cases, comparing the XLA implementations, TEPL rules with explicit RHS
attribute calculations, and TEPL rules with axis inference
(Table~\ref{tab:xla-specification-size}). Counts are grouped by responsibility
and exclude blank lines and comments.
Appendix~\ref{app:token-counting} gives an explicit/inferred rule pair and
its token counts by responsibility.

Across these cases, inferred TEPL uses 1,829 tokens, 80.1\% fewer than XLA
and 17.8\% fewer than explicit TEPL. Most savings over explicit TEPL come
from attribute reasoning and syntax, reduced from 554 to 191 tokens
(65.5\%). Inference removes 47 explicit RHS descriptors while preserving
the rewrite equations and conditions; legality checks and graph-management
code remain unchanged. Some XLA excerpts also implement additional cases and
policies, which contribute to their larger size.

The comparison with XLA reflects the broader benefit of expressing pattern
matching, attribute calculations, and replacement construction as
declarative rules.
