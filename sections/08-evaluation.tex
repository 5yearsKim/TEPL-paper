\section{XLA Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
We evaluate TEPL on 209 concrete rewrite cases adapted from XLA's algebraic
simplifier, measuring operation-semantic coverage, verification with explicit
RHS attributes, and automatic attribute inference. Counts refer to concrete
cases, which may specialize broader catalog rules. Table~\ref{tab:xla-results}
summarizes the three stages; their denominators differ as explained below.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{XLA case coverage and conditional attribute inference.}
\label{tab:xla-results}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}\toprule
Stage & Result\\\midrule
Semantic coverage & 118/209\\
Explicit verification & 86/118\\
Auto inference (axis/extent) & 20/20 (100\%)\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Semantic coverage.}
A case is eligible for verification when every required operation has a
semantic definition in TEPL. This holds for 118 of the 209 cases; the remaining
91 lack at least one required definition. Axis bindings, scalar expressions,
reductions, and calculated operand reads describe value computations and
supported coordinate transformations: transpose permutes axes, dot sums
products over contraction axes, and static slice computes coordinates from
starts and strides.

Supported operations include elementwise arithmetic, transpose,
axis-inserting broadcast, sum reduction, dot, reverse, and static slice with
positive sizes and strides. Convolution uses fixed layouts, positive strides
and dilations, and no padding or groups. General reshape, pad, concatenate,
and dynamic slice require coordinate capabilities beyond current semantic
support, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Explicit verification.}
We first supply RHS attributes through captured descriptors or handwritten
constructions, without \code{auto}. TEPL checks evaluability, RHS legality,
and agreement in output shape and values under the declared guards and
arithmetic policy. The recorded full-corpus sweep verifies 86 of the 118
eligible cases.

The other 32 remain unproved. This does not establish that their rewrites
are incorrect: TEPL may lack the required shape, axis, index, or scalar
reasoning, or the rule may need an additional applicability condition.
Successful verification is relative to the declared operation semantics and
arithmetic policy, not every aspect of XLA's numerical behavior.

\paragraph{Automatic inference.}
Of the 86 verified cases, 63 have no RHS attributes to infer, and three
require scalar settings such as comparison direction. The remaining 20
require only axis or extent lists, including permutations, reduction
dimensions, broadcast dimensions and extents, and slice parameters.

For these 20, we replace RHS attribute specifications with \code{auto} and
remove the corresponding handwritten constructions, retaining the same
patterns, guards, and operation semantics. TEPL constructs every required
attribute and verifies each completed rule in a fresh context. All 20 succeed
without RHS attribute hints, including descriptor reuse and computed programs.

Automatic inference succeeds on all 20 explicitly verified axis/extent cases.
These constitute the verified subset of 38 semantically eligible axis/extent
cases.

\paragraph{Relation to the theorem.}
These measurements evaluate the implementation's broader supported fragment,
not completeness of the mathematical algorithm. In particular, broadcast
and slice cases lie outside Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-synthesis}. The current
corpus results do not separately classify every case against the theorem's
structural, extent, and attribute restrictions. The 20/20 result remains
conditional on explicit verification; it does not establish completeness
of symbolic inference or guard reasoning.
