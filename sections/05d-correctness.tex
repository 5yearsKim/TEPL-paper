\subsection{Correctness and Scope}
\label{sec:correctness}
Synthesis proposes attributes; mapping queries alone do not establish their
evaluability, operation legality, or tensor equivalence.

\subsubsection{Checking the Completed Rewrite}
The verifier substitutes the emitted programs and reconstructs the completed
rule in a fresh context. Table~\ref{tab:verification-checks} separates the
four requirements. Reconstructing the rule means deriving its coordinate
relationships and values from the emitted programs, instead of assuming
the intermediate conclusions used during synthesis.
\begin{table}[!htbp]
\caption{Checks required for a completed semantic rewrite.}
\label{tab:verification-checks}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lY@{}}\toprule
Check & Required property\\\midrule
Attribute computation & Every constructor uses only source metadata, has no
holes, and is evaluable whenever the rule applies.\\
RHS legality & Constructed attributes satisfy operation contracts, including
valid positions, paired extents, reduction domains, and access bounds.\\
Value equivalence & Fully lowered expressions agree in indexed reads,
coefficients, and reduction domains under the selected arithmetic policy.\\
Output agreement & Results have identical ordered output shapes and
compatible fixed dtype.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
\FloatBarrier
Read matching against a skeleton assists construction but does not establish
value equivalence. Explicit attribute programs receive the same checks.
Only source legality and the declared guard may be assumed; additional
construction conditions must follow from them.

\subsubsection{Correctness Guarantee}
\label{sec:guarantee}
The intended guarantee is Equation~\eqref{eq:contract} for every legal guarded
source instance and every compatible tensor value, under the selected
arithmetic policy. Strict floating-point equivalence does not follow from
algebraic reassociation. This contract is not a soundness theorem established
in this draft, and the current C++ implementation is not claimed formally
proved sound.

Assumptions include correct operation definitions, sound primitive metadata
and index checks, admissible coordinate frames, and faithful evaluation of
partial attribute programs. Checked arithmetic, coordinate transport, and
guard evaluation are implementation obligations. Fresh-context checking
separates verification from construction conclusions, but both paths share
semantic infrastructure; that separation alone establishes no implementation
soundness result.

\subsubsection{Scope and Limitations}
The method is incomplete. Ambiguous read correspondence, unsupported coupled
or inverse equations, executable expressions beyond the reasoning fragment,
unavailable arithmetic laws, and bounded normalization or coordinate work
can prevent acceptance. Several read-coordinate assignments must remain
unresolved unless agreement is established. TEPL claims neither all valid
attribute programs nor weakest sufficient guards.

Diagnostics should distinguish malformed rules, unsupported or unresolved
reasoning, and exhausted work budgets. Rejection as unproved is not a
counterexample. Structural rules receive structural checks and do not carry
the intended semantic-equivalence guarantee.
