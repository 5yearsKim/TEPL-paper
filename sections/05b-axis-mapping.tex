\subsection{Symbolic Axis Mapping}
\label{sec:axis-mapping}
Mapping connects groups introduced by different operations through shared
tensor interfaces, preserving coordinate order and operand pairing.

\subsubsection{Building and Composing Mapping Tables}
\label{sec:mapping-tables}
A record $(\mathsf{I},G,q)$ satisfies Equation~\eqref{eq:mapping}. Mapping tables use
tensor columns and group rows; entries are position expressions, and a dash
indicates no mapping in that example. Write $\iota(G)=\operatorname{range}(|G|)$.
Let $b_\ell,b_r$ and $k_\ell,k_r$ denote the consuming dot's batch and
contraction lists, and $f_\ell,f_r$ their ordered complements.
An operand's groups need not be contiguous. For example, its axes might
appear as $[i,k_0,b,k_1]$, even though we describe its groups as $B,K,I$.
The compiler reconstructs dimension order using the binding positions:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat S=\gath(B\catseq K\catseq I,
             \operatorname{inverse}(b_\ell\catseq k_\ell\catseq f_\ell)).
 \label{eq:rebuilt-interface}
\end{equation}
In this example the concatenated positions are $[2,1,3,0]$; their inverse
reorders $[b,k_0,k_1,i]$ back to $[i,k_0,b,k_1]$. A valid complete binding
makes the position list a permutation, so the inverse exists. This step
changes the coordinate description used by the compiler while preserving
the tensor's dimension order. An existing interface can be reused when it
already satisfies the bindings.

\begin{figuretext}{figures/Symbolic Axis Mapping.pdf}{Shared tensor interfaces connect local axis descriptions without changing tensor values or physical axis order.}{fig:axis-mapping}{Local dot and addition mapping tables are connected through their shared
intermediate, producing a table whose axes use a common coordinate description.}
Figure~\ref{fig:axis-mapping} connects local descriptions for
\code{T=dot(X,Y); O=add(T,Z)}. Dot describes $T$ using $I\catseq J$;
addition calls the same axes $S$. Their shared interface aligns these
coordinates without changing tensor values or physical axis order.

For the distribution example, the composition is reversed:
\code{T=Add(X,Y); O=dot(T,Z)}. Addition initially gives $X,Y,T$ interface $S$.
The dot's batch, contraction, and free bindings refine that description.
Because addition preserves axis order, the dot's positions in $T$ also locate
the corresponding axes in $X$ and $Y$. The resulting output interface is
$B\catseq I\catseq J$. The table below makes this propagation explicit.
\end{figuretext}
In the distribution example, addition's output $T$ has the same axis order as $X$ and $Y$, so a position
selected in $T$ selects the corresponding coordinate in either input:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}\toprule
Group & $X$ & $Y$ & $T$ & $Z$ & $O$\\\midrule
$B$ & $b_\ell$ & $b_\ell$ & $b_\ell$ & $b_r$ & $\iota(B)$\\
$K$ & $k_\ell$ & $k_\ell$ & $k_\ell$ & $k_r$ & --\\
$I$ & $f_\ell$ & $f_\ell$ & $f_\ell$ & -- & $|B|+\iota(I)$\\
$J$ & -- & -- & -- & $f_r$ & $|B|+|I|+\iota(J)$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each entry is a position list, not an axis size. Adding a group length shifts
every position in a list. The $K$ row connects the coordinates summed over
in all three input tensors; its absence from $O$ records that the dot removes
them. This is the correspondence needed to compose the value expressions in
Section~\ref{sec:composition}.

Mappings compose by selecting positions through existing positions:
\begin{equation}
 \gath(\gath(\mathsf{I},q),h)=\gath(\mathsf{I},\gath(q,h)).
 \label{eq:gather-composition}
\end{equation}
Thus $(\mathsf{I},G,q)$ and $(G,H,h)$ locate $H$ in $\mathsf{I}$ at $\gath(q,h)$.
Lookup can derive such relationships on demand rather than enumerating every
possible mapping. Composition through a producer with several groups also
requires recording which portions a consumer selects.

\subsubsection{Symbolic Axis Partitioning}
\label{sec:partitioning}
For reassociation $(XY)Z\to X(YZ)$, let the inner source dot produce
$T$ with $\mathsf{I}_T=B\catseq I\catseq J$, contracting $K$. The rule's guard
\code{@outer.lhs_batching_dimensions == @inner->B} requires the outer
left batch list to select the inner batch group in the same order.
Incompatible routing can change the result; this guard restricts applicability
without replacing completed-rule verification.

The outer dot contracts group $D$ through lists
$k_\ell^{\mathrm{outer}},k_r^{\mathrm{outer}}$. Its left list can interleave
selections from $I$ and $J$. Figure~\ref{fig:axis-partitioning} shows why
three positions must remain distinct: within the producer group, within the
contraction list, and in the paired operand.

\paperfigure{1}{figures/Symbolic Axis Partitioning (Dot Reassociation).pdf}{Partitioning selected axes by producer origin preserves their contraction partners for reassociation.}{fig:axis-partitioning}{Three panels show selecting producer output axes, splitting them by origin,
and retaining contraction partners, followed by a subgroup mapping table.}
For $G\in\{I,J\}$, let $q_G$ locate $G$ in $T$ and compute
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 (u_G,v_G)&=\mpo(k_\ell^{\mathrm{outer}},q_G),\\
 G_{\mathrm{sel}}&=\gath(G,v_G),\qquad
 G_{\mathrm{rem}}=\gath(G,\ocomp(\iota(G),v_G)),\\
 z_G&=\gath(k_r^{\mathrm{outer}},u_G).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:z-partners}
\end{equation}
\code{matchPositions(a,b)} scans distinct-entry list $a$ in order and returns
positions $u,v$ satisfying $\gath(a,u)=\gath(b,v)$. Here $u_G$ records
contraction slots, $v_G$ positions within $G$, and $z_G$ the paired positions
in $Z$. Selected axes retain contraction order; remaining axes retain producer
order, so their concatenation need not reconstruct $G$.
In the figure, left selection $[j_1,i_0]$ pairs with $Z$ positions $[2,0]$:
$j_1$ uses slot 0 and $i_0$ slot 1. The refined source output is
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{I}_O=B\catseq\Irem\catseq\Jrem\catseq H.
 \label{eq:source-output}
\end{equation}
The figure is unbatched; these equations retain $B$ for the guarded case.

For the figure's selection, $i_0$ is at position $0$ within $I$ but at slot
$1$ in the outer contraction list. Thus $v_I=[0]$ and $u_I=[1]$.
Selecting slot $1$ from the right contraction list $[2,0]$ gives $z_I=[0]$.
Likewise, $j_1$ gives $v_J=[1]$, $u_J=[0]$, and $z_J=[2]$.
Using a within-group position to select from the right contraction list
would lose this pairing. The distinction remains necessary when the lists
have symbolic lengths and cannot be enumerated during rule compilation.

The new mappings connect each selected subgroup both to its producer and
to $Z$. For example, if $i_X$ locates $I$ in $X$, then
$\gath(i_X,v_I)$ locates $\Isel$ in $X$, and $z_I$ locates its partners
in $Z$. These entries will let synthesis construct a new contraction even
though $X$ and $Z$ were not directly paired by the source inner dot.
Appendix~\ref{app:mapping} gives the complete refined table.

\subsubsection{General Construction Algorithm}
\label{sec:mapping-algorithm}
Construction processes producers before consumers. Its state
$\Sigma=(V,\mathcal M,\Pi)$ records each tensor's interface and value expression in
$V[t]=(\mathsf{I}_t,e_t)$, mappings in $\mathcal M$, and complete disjoint partitions in $\Pi$.
For every mapping,
\begin{equation}
 (\mathsf{I},G,q)\in \mathcal M\ \Longrightarrow\ \gath(\mathsf{I},q)=G.
 \label{eq:mapping-invariant}
\end{equation}
For a partition $\langle(G_1,q_1),\ldots,(G_m,q_m)\rangle$ of $\mathsf{I}$, every
member satisfies that invariant and $q_1\catseq\cdots\catseq q_m$ is a
permutation of $\iota(\mathsf{I})$. Different partitions may overlap.

The distinction between $\mathcal M$ and $\Pi$ matters. If addition describes all of
$T$ by $S=[i,j]$, $\mathcal M$ locates $S$ at $[0,1]$, and $\Pi$ records that $S$
covers the interface. A consumer may describe the same axes by $I=[i]$ and
$J=[j]$. The compiler adds their individual positions to $\mathcal M$ and their joint
coverage to $\Pi$, retaining both descriptions. The shape and axis order of
$T$ stay unchanged.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:mapping} summarizes construction. Resolving a binding
establishes its position validity and paired extents. Rebuilding an interface
uses the inverse-position construction in Equation~\eqref{eq:rebuilt-interface}.
Refinement compares partitions on the same interface and adds mappings for
their overlaps. Transport expresses the producer's indexed reads in the
consumer's coordinate description, so that composing their value expressions
also composes the corresponding accesses.
\begin{paperalgorithm}[H]
\caption{Build interfaces, value expressions, and axis mappings.}
\label{alg:mapping}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
def build_symbolic_axis_mapping(graph):
    V = initialize_inputs(graph)
    mappings, partitions = set(), {}
    for op in producer_first(graph):
        inputs = [V[t] for t in op.inputs]
        groups, bindings = resolve_bindings(op, inputs)
        aligned = []
        for (axes, value), binding in zip(inputs, bindings):
            aligned_axes = build_interface(axes, binding)
            register_mappings(aligned_axes, binding, mappings)
            refine_and_register(aligned_axes, binding, mappings, partitions)
            value = transport(value, axes, aligned_axes, mappings, partitions)
            aligned.append((aligned_axes, value))
        out_axes = evaluate(op.output_axes, groups)
        binding = output_positions(op, groups)
        register_mappings(out_axes, binding, mappings)
        refine_and_register(out_axes, binding, mappings, partitions)
        V[op] = (out_axes, compose(op.value, aligned, groups))
    return V, mappings, partitions
\end{lstlisting}
\end{paperalgorithm}
Here \code{register_mappings} adds an entry for each group and its positions;
\code{aligned} holds each input interface and its transported value expression. The output
interface and mappings follow from the operation's declared output order.
Later queries can derive additional mappings using
Equation~\eqref{eq:gather-composition}. Appendix~\ref{app:mapping} explains
the helper operations in detail. These construction invariants do not
replace verification of the completed rewrite.
