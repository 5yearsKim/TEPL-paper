\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
TEPL is a DSL for writing readable tensor rewrite rules and applying them in
practice. Using Axes IR and symbolic axis mapping, it generates programs at
compile time to compute RHS attributes, reducing manual attribute
transformation logic. We prove constructive completeness for a restricted
set of operations and rewrite structures: under the stated assumptions,
synthesis finds correct attributes whenever they exist.

The implementation also supports rewrites outside this fragment, checking
synthesized candidates with an independent verifier under the declared
algebraic semantics and written conditions. Coverage remains limited,
particularly for general indexing and richer reductions.

In our XLA evaluation, TEPL models the operation semantics required by 123
of 209 cases and synthesizes attributes for 31 of 38 eligible cases (81.6\%).
Integration into XLA's existing simplifier demonstrates practical
application. Across 25 audited cases, inference and its permitted cleanup
reduce TEPL token counts by 17.8\% compared with explicit RHS attributes.
These results show how declarative tensor rewrites can delegate attribute
construction to the compiler.
