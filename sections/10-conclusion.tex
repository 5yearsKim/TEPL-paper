\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
TEPL is a DSL for writing readable tensor rewrite rules and applying them
in practice. At compile time, it generates programs to compute RHS
attributes, reducing the manual attribute transformation logic. We prove
constructive completeness for a restricted set of operations and rewrite
structures: under the stated assumptions, our synthesis algorithm finds
correct attributes whenever they exist. To integrate attribute synthesis
into the language, we propose Axes IR and symbolic axis mapping, which
represent operation semantics and track coordinate relationships, enabling
the compiler to construct RHS attribute programs.

The current implementation also supports rewrites outside the completeness
fragment. For these additional rewrites, an
independent verifier checks soundness under the declared semantics and
written conditions. Coverage remains limited, particularly for tensor
operations involving general indexing or general reductions.

In our XLA evaluation, TEPL supports the operation semantics required by
123 of 209 concrete rewrite cases and successfully infers and verifies
31 of the 38 cases requiring axis inference (81.6\%). Across the 31
successful cases, inferred TEPL uses 80.1\% fewer tokens than the
corresponding XLA implementations and 17.8\% fewer than explicit TEPL.
These results show how declarative rewrite rules and automatic axis
inference can reduce the manual work required to implement tensor rewrites.
