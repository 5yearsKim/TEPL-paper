\subsection{Soundness of Additional Rewrite Schemas}
\label{app:soundness-of-additional-rewrite-schemas}
This appendix proves additional tensor rewrites correct under explicit conditions.

\paperparagraph{Notation and assumptions.}
All source selectors are distinct and in range.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Broadcast} $B_{d,e}X$ reads $X[z[d]]$:
$d$ gives the original dimensions' output positions, and $e$
lists inserted extents in output order.
\item \textbf{Transpose} $T_\phi X$ reads $X[z[\phi^{-1}]]$.
\item \textbf{Reversal} $R_AX$ replaces $z_i$ with $n_i-1-z_i$
for $i\in A$, where $n_i$ is the axis extent.
\item \textbf{Reduction} $\Sigma_AX$ sums over $A$,
preserving the remaining axis order.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of additional rewrites]
\label{lem:certified-extensions}
For each rewrite below, the stated conditions ensure that the
constructed replacement is legal and preserves the output shape,
axis order, and values. This holds for arbitrary finite ranks and
nonnegative extents under exact algebraic semantics, assuming
the stated operation contracts.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We show that each replacement reads the same input entries or computes
the same finite sum, grouping rewrites by operation.

\paperparagraph{View simplification.}
A chain of reversals becomes one reversal on the symmetric difference
of its axis sets: reversing twice cancels, and reversals on different
axes commute. An empty reversal can be removed.

For nested broadcasts $B_{b,f}(B_{d,e}X)$, require
$b=[0,\ldots,r+|e|-1]$, where $r$ is the input rank, so the outer broadcast
only appends dimensions. The replacement $B_{\gath(b,d),e\catseq f}X$
preserves the input reads and output shape.

For the singleton-axis view, require
$\operatorname{shape}(X)=[1]\catseq t$ and $d=[0,\ldots,r-1]$,
where $r=\operatorname{rank}(X)$.
Remove input dimension zero and broadcast the remaining tensor with
embedding $[1,\ldots,r-1]$ and inserted extents $[1]\catseq e$.
Both results have shape $[1]\catseq t\catseq e$ and read the same entry
because the removed coordinate is always zero.

\paperparagraph{Operations through broadcast.}
For $\star\in\{+,\times\}$, matching embeddings and inserted extents give
$(B_{d,e}X)\star(B_{d,e}Y)=B_{d,e}(X\star Y)$.
Source legality implies equal input shapes, and both sides compute
$X[z[d]]\star Y[z[d]]$.

For reversal, set $Q=\{j\mid d_j\in A\}$. Then
$R_A(B_{d,e}X)=B_{d,e}(R_QX)$: reversing an inserted dimension cannot
change the input read; the other reversals act on the corresponding input
axes. If $Q=\varnothing$, including scalar broadcast with $d=[]$, omit
the input reversal.

For transpose, let $t=\operatorname{shape}(B_{d,e}X)$ and $n=|t|$.
Construct
\[
 d'=\gath(\phi^{-1},d),\qquad
 e'=\gath(\gath(t,\phi),\ocomp([0,\ldots,n-1],d')).
\]
Here $d'$ gives the original dimensions' transposed positions, and $e'$
lists the remaining extents in output order. Thus $B_{d',e'}X$ preserves
the shape and reads of $T_\phi(B_{d,e}X)$.
For scalar broadcast, $d'=[]$ and
$e'=\gath(e,\phi)$. A broadcast inserting no dimensions is itself the
transpose $T_{d^{-1}}X$.

For reduction, require $A\subseteq\operatorname{im}(d)$, so only original
dimensions are reduced. Set $Q=\{j\mid d_j\in A\}$ and, for
each $j\notin Q$, retain the embedding entry
$d_j-|\{a\in A\mid a<d_j\}|$. These entries form $d'$ in input order,
giving $\Sigma_A(B_{d,e}X)=B_{d',e}(\Sigma_QX)$.
The subtraction shifts positions past removed dimensions, and both sides
sum over the same input coordinates. Reducing an inserted dimension is
excluded because it would add a multiplicity factor.

\paperparagraph{Scalar identities.}
Reversal distributes over addition because both sides add the same
reversed entries; a broadcast scalar is unchanged by reversal.
Repeated negation depends only on parity, and negation commutes with
finite summation, including an empty sum.

For scalar $c$, bilinearity gives
$\operatorname{dot}(cX,Y)=c\operatorname{dot}(X,Y)$, and similarly on the
right. Copy the dot's axis fields and broadcast $c$ to its output shape.
For nested products, the identity
\[
 (X\cdot B_{[],\operatorname{shape}(X)}c)\cdot B_{d,e}Y
 =X\cdot B_{d,e}(Y\cdot B_{[],\operatorname{shape}(Y)}c)
\]
follows from associativity and commutativity at each coordinate;
source legality ensures matching shapes.

\paperparagraph{Dot rewrites.}
To move reversal from one operand to the other, require every reversed
position in $A$ to belong to that operand's contracting list $k$.
If $h$ is the other contracting list, reverse positions
$\gath(h,\{j\mid k_j\in A\})$ there and copy all dot axis fields.
Paired extents are equal, so changing each selected summation index
from $t$ to $n-1-t$ preserves the sum and leaves output coordinates unchanged.

With no batch axes and both contracting lists equal to their full input
position ranges, dot is the pointwise product of equal-shaped operands
followed by reduction over all dimensions.
With all four axis lists empty, dot is an outer product.
Broadcast the left operand into the output prefix and the right operand
into the output suffix, inserting the other operand's shape in each case,
then multiply. This preserves the dot's left-free/right-free output order.

\paperparagraph{Broadcast-created contractions.}
Let $d$ embed $X$ into the left operand, and let $I$ be the inserted
positions. Require every position in $I$ to belong to the left contracting
list. Reduce $Y$ over the right contracting positions paired with $I$,
then remove those pairs from the dot. Map the remaining left batch and
contracting positions back through $d$. Shift each surviving right position
to account for the preceding dimensions removed by the reduction.

Require left batch positions to be embedded original positions and the
mapped left free positions to preserve the source's free output order.
The removed right positions are distinct and disjoint from retained batch
and contracting positions. The replacement therefore preserves legality
and output order. At each output coordinate,
\[
 \sum_{a,b}X[a]Y[a,b]=\sum_a X[a]\left(\sum_bY[a,b]\right),
\]
where $b$ ranges over the broadcast-created contracting dimensions.
This is finite-sum distribution, including empty summation domains.

\paperparagraph{Legality and boundary cases.}
The stated conditions keep selectors distinct and in range, batch and
contracting roles disjoint, and output shapes and axis orders unchanged.
Identical operation trees may copy source attributes: induction over
matching nodes preserves legality and results. Supplied fields proved
equal to constructed fields under the effective applicability condition
also give the same replacement.
Zero extents give no output coordinates or empty sums; on singleton axes,
reversal is the identity and the only coordinate is zero.
Thus the listed rewrites remain sound for all nonnegative extents.
\end{proof}
