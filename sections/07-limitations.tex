\subsection{Coverage}
\label{sec:coverage}

\paragraph{Can TEPL cover all tensor operations?}
In principle, yes. Users can declare operations in a dialect and manually
specify attribute transformations and applicability conditions using
\code{derive} and \code{where}.
Operations need not have Axes IR semantics, but users must ensure
correctness when those semantics are absent.

\paragraph{Can TEPL infer all attributes?}
No. TEPL synthesizes only eligible RHS \code{axis} fields.
Extent and coordinate fields must be supplied explicitly or through
schema defaults. Supporting an operation's semantics does not imply
that TEPL can infer all of its attributes: for example, static slice
semantics are supported, but slice offsets, strides, and target
extents are not inferred.

Axis synthesis also requires operation semantics supported by Axes IR,
and this support alone does not guarantee successful inference.
Axes IR supports axis relationships and limited explicit coordinate
calculations, but cannot currently represent accesses through
tensor-valued indices, such as dynamic slice's runtime start indices.
Table~\ref{tab:semantic-coverage} distinguishes semantic support
from theorem scope.

\paragraph{Is axis inference complete for every supported operation?}
No. Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-synthesis} guarantees complete axis
inference under the restrictions in Section~\ref{sec:formal-scope}.
TEPL also supports operations outside this scope, where inference
and verification may fail.
Accepted rewrites must produce legal, equivalent replacements
under the written conditions and arithmetic policy.
Outside the guaranteed scope, TEPL may verify a rewrite with
user-provided attributes even when it cannot infer those attributes
automatically.

\subsection{Scalability}
We assess scalability along two dimensions: the number of operations in a
rewrite chain and tensor rank. Longer chains require reasoning across more
operations, while higher ranks increase the number of axes involved.
We measure how attribute construction and verification time changes along
each dimension. Appendix~\ref{app:scalability} presents the experiments,
results, and measurement limits.
