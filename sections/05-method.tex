\section{From Mathematical Synthesis to Symbolic Programs}
\label{sec:method}
The preceding sections define and prove a mathematical synthesis algorithm.
Its completeness guarantee does not extend to the symbolic implementation:
attribute construction, guard proving, and condition suggestions may remain
unresolved even for valid rewrites.
However, the mathematical algorithm selects logical axes for given
metadata, while the compiler must generate attribute programs before
tensor ranks and axis positions are known. The challenge is to express
these selections and orderings as computations over source metadata.

TEPL bridges this gap through three mechanisms. Axes IR represents varying
numbers of axes as ordered symbolic groups. Symbolic axis mapping connects
these groups across operations while preserving their positions and pairings.
Attribute program synthesis uses these mappings to generate expressions
that compute replacement attributes from source metadata.
At each match, evaluating these expressions on concrete metadata produces
the positional attribute lists.
